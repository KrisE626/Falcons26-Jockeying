What is jockeying?

Jockeying is how the 1st defender (the player closest to the ball) slows an attacker down, keeps them in front, and steers them where we want them to go, until it's the right moment to win the ball.

In our 3-5-2, every Falcon is a defender when we lose the ball. If you're closest, you're the 1st defender. That means the 9 and 10 jockey too.

DelaySlow them down so teammates can recover.
DenyBlock the path to our goal.
DirectShow them where to go: away from danger.

The 5 keys

Close down fast

When the ball travels to your player, sprint at them while the ball is rolling. Every yard you close now is a yard they can't use.

"Go while the ball travels"

Break down

In the last 3–4 yards, chop your steps, lower your hips and slow to a stop about an arm's length to 2 yards away. Arrive under control, not flying.

"Sprint, then chop"

Get side-on

One foot in front of the other, knees bent, weight on the balls of your feet, arms out for balance. Never stand square (feet level) — a square defender can be beaten both ways and through the legs.

"Low and side-on"

Show them one way

Stand slightly off-center so one side is blocked and one side looks open. Usually we show them outside, toward the sideline and away from our goal. Coach may tell you to show inside if our cover is there.

"Show them the line"

Be patient, watch the ball

Keep your eyes on the ball, not their feet or hips (that's where the fakes are). Shuffle and backpedal with them. Don't lunge. Wait for your moment.

"Stay on your feet"

When to win it

Jockeying isn't just backing up forever. These are your triggers to step in and tackle:

  • ✓Heavy touch. The ball gets away from their feet. Pounce.
  • ✓Head down or back turned. They can't see you coming. Get tight.
  • ✓Trapped by the line. You've shown them to the sideline and they've run out of room.
  • ✓Cover arrives. When a teammate calls "I'm with you," you can be more aggressive. If you miss, they're behind you.

When you tackle, use your front foot, keep your weight forward and stay on your feet. Slide only as the last option.

Common mistakes

  • ✕Diving in. One lunge and they're past you, with nobody behind.
  • ✕Arriving at full speed. You can't stop or turn. They just touch it by you.
  • ✕Standing square and tall. Slow to turn and easy to nutmeg.
  • ✕Too far away. More than 3 yards gives them time to look up and pass or shoot.
  • ✕Buying the fake. Watching their hips or step-overs instead of the ball.
  • ✕Crossing your feet. Shuffle, don't run sideways with crossed legs.

Homework drills

Do all three at least twice before next week's practice. Use cones, shoes or water bottles as markers.

1 · Shadow footwork Solo · 5 min

  1. Get side-on: feet staggered, knees bent, on your toes.
  2. Shuffle backward 5 yards, staying low. Don't cross your feet.
  3. Drop-step: swing your hips and shuffle the other way.
  4. Sprint 5 yards forward, then chop your steps to a stop in your stance.

Do 4 rounds of 30 seconds, 30 seconds rest.

0:30

2 · Mirror channel Partner · 5 min

  1. Make a channel about 5 yards wide and 10 yards long.
  2. Partner (sibling, parent, friend) dribbles slowly side to side and forward.
  3. You stay 1–2 yards away, side-on, eyes on the ball. No tackling. Just keep them in front.
  4. Switch roles every 30 seconds.

No partner? Do it without a ball: someone walks/jogs randomly and you mirror them.

3 · Show them the line Partner · 5 min

  1. Set up two small goals (gates) at the far end: one on the left, one on the right.
  2. Before each turn, pick which gate you'll allow. Block the other.
  3. Partner starts 10 yards away. Pass them the ball, then sprint and break down.
  4. Win a point if they go through the gate you allowed, or if you win the ball on a bad touch.

Play first to 5, then switch.
